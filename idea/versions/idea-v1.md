# Café rota

## The problem

A café with eight part-time staff plans shifts in a group chat. Nobody is sure who is on when, swaps get agreed in private messages, and the manager finds out when somebody does not turn up.

## Who has it

The café manager, who plans the week, and the staff, who need to know their own shifts and sometimes swap one.

## How they cope today

A photo of a paper rota posted in a group chat every Sunday, and private messages for swaps.

## What else already does this

- Deputy — https://www.deputy.com — rostering for larger businesses, priced per person. (unverified)
- The group chat.

## What is different about yours

Built for one small café: the manager plans the week in a few minutes, staff see only their own shifts on their phone, and a swap is asked for and approved in the app, so the manager always knows who is really working.

## The core flows

1. The manager signs in, adds staff members (name and email, with a starting password the staff member changes when they first sign in), and removes someone who has left.
2. The manager plans a week: adds shifts (day, start time, end time, who is working), and publishes the week when it is ready. Staff cannot see a week until it is published.
3. A staff member signs in and sees only their own published shifts, soonest first.
4. A staff member asks to swap one of their shifts with a named colleague. The manager approves or turns down the request, and the rota changes only when it is approved.

## What is in the first version, and what is not

In: sign-in for the manager and staff, staff accounts, planning and publishing a week, each person's own shifts, swap requests and approval, all saved in the database. Out: pay and hours totals, text or email reminders, clocking in, more than one café.

## The riskiest assumptions

- Staff will check the app instead of the group chat. Test: run one month with the app only.
